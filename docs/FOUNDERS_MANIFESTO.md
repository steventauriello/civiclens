# Founder's Manifesto

Government information should not be understandable only to government.

Everyday Americans fund public institutions through taxes, fees, utility payments, assessments, and other charges. They deserve more than a stack of PDFs and a promise that the numbers are correct. They deserve the ability to follow the money, examine the evidence, understand the results, and ask informed questions.

CivicLens begins with a simple belief:

> Public information is not truly transparent unless the public can understand and verify it.

## Why CivicLens exists

Technology has given large institutions enormous analytical power. Artificial intelligence can now help ordinary citizens work through records that once required teams of accountants, attorneys, analysts, and researchers.

CivicLens will use that leverage responsibly. The purpose is not to manufacture outrage or assume wrongdoing. The purpose is to make the facts accessible enough that responsible government can be recognized and poor stewardship can be challenged with evidence.

## Our commitments

We will follow the records wherever they lead.

We will not manipulate the analysis to protect a party, official, agency, contractor, ideology, or founder's preferred conclusion.

We will distinguish:

- Budgets from actual spending
- Appropriations from payments
- City taxes from county, school, and special-district taxes
- General revenue from restricted funds
- Expenses from transfers
- Operating costs from capital costs
- Evidence from inference
- Missing information from proof of wrongdoing

We will say “the available records do not establish that” when the evidence is incomplete.

We will make corrections visible.

We will preserve source documents and the path used to produce each answer.

## The public-dollar standard

Our ambition is to follow every public dollar. Our promise must remain precise:

> CivicLens will trace every public dollar that can be supported by available official records, and it will clearly identify where the public record stops.

## The AEN standard

Before taxpayers are asked for more money, they should be able to examine three questions:

1. **Accountability:** Is the money already collected protected, authorized, traceable, and responsibly managed?
2. **Effectiveness:** Are the funded programs producing the results they were created to achieve?
3. **Need:** Is additional funding genuinely necessary, or is reform the better answer?

CivicLens will not force the verdict. It will organize the evidence.

## Citizens first

The primary user is not an accountant or software engineer. It is the resident asking a normal question:

- Why did my taxes increase?
- Where did the money go?
- What did this project cost?
- Who approved it?
- Did it work?

If CivicLens cannot answer clearly, show its evidence, and explain its limitations, it is not finished.
