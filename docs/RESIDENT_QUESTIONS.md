# Questions Residents Should Be Able to Ask

This list guides data collection and product scope. Questions are organized by what evidence is required, not by what is easiest to display.

## First ten alpha questions

1. How much total revenue did Port St. Lucie report last fiscal year?
2. How much came from property taxes?
3. Where did the City's spending go by fund and department?
4. What was budgeted versus actually spent?
5. Which departments increased spending the most compared with the prior year?
6. How much did the City spend on police, parks, roads, utilities, and administration?
7. What major capital projects were funded, and what is their status?
8. Which vendors received the largest payments or contracts?
9. How much debt does the City report, and what is it for?
10. What evidence exists that a selected program or project passes an AEN Review?

## Property-tax questions

- What portion of my tax bill belongs to the City rather than the county, schools, or special districts?
- What was the City millage rate this year and last year?
- Did my City tax increase because of the rate, taxable value, or both?
- How much property-tax revenue did the City budget and actually recognize?
- What percentage of City revenue came from property taxes?
- How would a stated amount of City property tax be allocated across services using TRACE' published estimate method?
- Which services are funded primarily by other revenue sources?

## Revenue questions

- What are the City's largest revenue sources?
- How much came from utility charges, impact fees, grants, state revenue, federal revenue, permits, fines, or investment income?
- Which revenue is restricted to a specific purpose?
- How much money came from borrowing rather than recurring revenue?
- How much revenue was transferred between funds?
- Which revenue sources were above or below budget?

## Spending questions

- What did each department spend?
- What categories grew the fastest?
- How much was spent on personnel, contracts, supplies, equipment, capital projects, and debt service?
- Which funds ended the year under or over budget?
- How much was encumbered but not yet paid?
- What spending was one-time versus recurring?
- Which spending was supported by grants or restricted revenue?

## Vendor and contract questions

- Who are the City's largest vendors?
- How much was each vendor paid by year?
- What contracts are active with a vendor?
- Which procurement method was used?
- Was the contract competitively bid, sole-sourced, or otherwise exempt?
- What amendments and change orders increased the value?
- Which department owns the contract?
- Who approved it and when?

## Project questions

- Which projects are active?
- What was the original and current budget?
- How much has actually been spent?
- Is the project on schedule?
- What caused material cost or schedule changes?
- What funding sources support it?
- Which contractors are involved?
- What outcome or service improvement was promised?

## Debt and reserve questions

- How much outstanding debt does the City report?
- What projects or obligations does the debt support?
- What are annual debt-service costs?
- What reserves and fund balances are available?
- Which reserves are legally or contractually restricted?
- How do current levels compare with policy targets?

## Accountability questions

- Can each major amount be traced to an official source?
- Was the spending authorized?
- Were procurement rules followed according to the available records?
- Did audits identify control problems or questioned costs?
- Were audit recommendations resolved?
- Are there unexplained variances or missing records?

## Effectiveness questions

- What result was the program supposed to produce?
- What measure and target were established?
- What result was reported?
- What did each unit of output or outcome cost?
- Did performance improve after spending increased?
- Are comparisons with prior years or similar cities available?

## Need questions

- What documented problem justified the funding?
- What existing capacity and funding were available?
- Was the need one-time or recurring?
- What alternatives were considered?
- What happens if the proposal is not funded?
- Could reform, reprioritization, management changes, or existing balances address the problem?

## Questions TRACE must answer cautiously

- “Was this fraud?” requires an authoritative finding, not an unexplained number.
- “Was this corrupt?” requires evidence beyond cost, delay, or political disagreement.
- “Where did my exact penny go?” may require an estimate because most tax revenue is pooled.
- “Did the program work?” cannot be answered without a defined objective and outcome evidence.

## Question acceptance template

Before adding a question to production, document:

- User intent
- Required datasets
- Calculation method
- Accounting qualifications
- Evidence requirements
- Expected answer structure
- Known limitations
- Test cases
